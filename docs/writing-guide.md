# The writing creed

Speak with purpose. Give the reader a task they can carry out. Make every claim
answer to evidence. Armorer's voice is spare, resolute and protective: the craft
of forging armor carried into technical instructions.

**This is the way.**

## Speak like an Armorer

Use short, direct sentences and active verbs. Address the reader as a capable
practitioner. Favor inspect, review, retain, bind, verify, preserve and forge.
Use duty and craft to frame the procedure. Let the technical details carry weight.

The Mandalorian influence belongs in the prose: an opening line, a firm boundary,
a transition after a completed procedure. Commands, flags, filenames, API names,
JSON keys and diagnostic codes remain exact. A reader must be able to follow the
guide without recognizing the inspiration.

Use **“This is the way.”** often. Place it after a review sequence, a preservation
rule, or a trust boundary, and at the close of a guide. Longer guides can repeat
it at several meaningful checkpoints. The phrase seals the instruction; the
instruction must still say what to do.

## Show the craft

| Plain draft | Armorer voice |
| --- | --- |
| Review the generated plan before applying it. | Inspect the plan. Retain the approved digest. Apply the exact intent. This is the way. |
| Existing modifications are preserved on conflict. | Your customizations stay yours. Resolve the conflict. Review a fresh plan. |
| Verification cannot continue without the required evidence. | Required evidence is missing. Hold the gate. |
| Readiness states are tracked independently. | Configuration, CI and release trust each need their own proof. Earn each claim. |

Metaphor introduces a duty. Concrete steps complete it. Give prerequisites before
commands, expected output after commands, and a recovery path when a procedure
can change files. Copyable examples need valid names and supported flags. Mark
placeholders plainly and test the walkthrough in a temporary workspace.

**This is the way.**

## Earn the claim

Identify whether a guide describes main, a named PR at an exact commit, an
accepted design, or dated research. Proposed commands belong in development
guides until their implementation lands. Link to the detailed contract rather
than duplicating its full rules across every page.

Distinguish schema validation, semantic consistency, genuine cryptographic
verification, hosted rehearsal, human acceptance and publication. Preserve
synthetic labels on examples. Keep the v0.1 SLSA Build L2 target separate from
demonstrated achievement; L3 stays future work.

The reader's trust rests on accurate claims. Preserve historical validation
receipts. Add new dated evidence with exact source identities. Update the current
behavior guide when behavior changes, and recheck every linked command.

**This is the way.**
